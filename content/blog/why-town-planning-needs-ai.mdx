India's planning system doesn't have a paperwork problem. It has a *reading comprehension* problem at scale — thousands of pages of acts, rules, circulars, and amendments that change by state, by municipality, and sometimes by ward.

## Automation solves the wrong layer

Most "digital governance" tools digitize the *process* — online forms instead of paper forms, a portal instead of a counter. That's real progress, but it doesn't touch the actual bottleneck: someone still has to know that a particular plot, in a particular zone, under a particular amendment, is subject to a 6-meter setback instead of 4.5.

## What a citation-first assistant changes

Town Planning AI doesn't just answer the question — it points at the exact section it came from. That's the difference between "trust me" and "verify me in thirty seconds."

For a planner working across multiple municipalities, that's not a convenience feature. It's the entire value proposition.

## Where this goes next

As Rule Search AI expands state coverage, the same citation-first approach extends across India — so the answer to "what's the setback requirement here" stops depending on who you ask, and starts depending on what the law actually says.
